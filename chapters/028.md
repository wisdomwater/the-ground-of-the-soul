## Day 28 - The Limits of Our Knowing

> *The highest understanding does not cling to what it knows, but presses beyond every name and image toward the source of truth itself.*

I tell you that knowledge and love are among the noblest powers of the soul, yet they do not approach God in quite the same manner. Love reaches toward God as goodness, finding delight in what is beautiful and worthy of desire. Understanding, however, is not satisfied even with goodness or beauty. It presses beyond every quality, seeking the divine being from which all goodness and beauty proceed.

Consider how readily you give names to the things you encounter. You call one thing good, another wise, and still another true. These names help you recognize the gifts of creation, but when you apply them to God, you must remember that his nature exceeds the meanings you have given them. Even your highest thoughts about God remain clothed in the forms of your understanding. If you would know him more deeply, you must allow the intellect to pass beyond these forms toward their hidden source.

Yet this inward journey requires more than the sharpening of your mind. The soul must also become free from the desires, fears, and cares that cloud its vision. I tell you that humility opens the way into the deepest ground, for the soul that descends beneath its own claims becomes receptive to the power of God. There, divine wisdom accomplishes what human understanding alone cannot achieve, drawing the soul toward a knowledge beyond its ordinary powers.

Therefore, seek truth diligently, but do not mistake your understanding for the fullness of truth. Let knowledge awaken wonder, and let wonder lead you into humility. For the soul that ceases to possess God through its ideas may become ready to receive him in the simplicity of his own being.
