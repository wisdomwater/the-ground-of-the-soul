## Day 33 - Blessed Are the Poor in Spirit

> *The deepest poverty is to possess nothing, not even the desire to possess God.*

When our Lord declared, “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven,” he spoke of a poverty far greater than the surrender of earthly possessions. Outward poverty may be a noble discipline, but I would lead you toward an inward poverty in which the soul becomes free from everything it claims as its own. I tell you that the truly poor person is one who wills nothing, knows nothing, and has nothing.

You may suppose that to will nothing means simply to surrender your desires and seek only the will of God. This is good, yet I speak of something deeper. As long as you cling to your desire to fulfill God's will, you still possess a will that you call your own. The deepest poverty releases even this claim, becoming so free that nothing stands between the soul and the divine life from which it came.

Likewise, to know nothing is not to become ignorant of the world or abandon wisdom. It is to cease possessing even your knowledge of God. Your understanding may hold many beautiful truths, yet the divine ground lies beyond everything you can comprehend. The soul must become free even from its awareness of its own spiritual progress, allowing God to work without demanding to observe or understand that work.

And to have nothing is to surrender even the inward place you have prepared for God. For if you claim to provide a dwelling in which God may act, something of yourself remains as its owner. Let God himself be the dwelling, the work, and the worker. In such poverty, the soul discovers a freedom beyond all striving, resting in the simplicity of the divine life.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Inhale:** *Empty me of all possession*
>
> **Exhale:** *Let You alone be my fullness*
