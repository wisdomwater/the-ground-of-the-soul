## Day 3 - The Silence We Cannot Manufacture

> *The soul does not bring forth God through its own effort, but becomes still enough to receive what God alone can give.*

You may ask what you must do to prepare yourself for the birth of the divine Word within your soul. Should you fill your mind with holy thoughts, contemplate the wisdom and goodness of God, or labor diligently to make yourself worthy of this gift? These are worthy questions, for the soul naturally desires to participate in its own transformation. Yet I tell you that the deepest work of God cannot be accomplished through the powers of your own understanding.

Consider how you approach the things of this world. You gather knowledge, form images, and exercise your will to bring about what you desire. Such efforts are useful in their proper place, but the divine birth belongs to another order of reality. God requires no instrument through which to accomplish his work, and no image through which to communicate his being. Where the soul has become free from its restless activity, God acts immediately and without mediation.

You may find this difficult, for even your desire to become still can become another form of striving. You begin to measure your silence, judge your progress, and wonder whether you have emptied yourself sufficiently. In this way, the soul remains occupied with its own activity, even while seeking to surrender it. True receptivity asks something simpler and more profound: that you release your need to govern the encounter and allow God to work according to his own nature.

Do not suppose that this makes your spiritual life passive or indifferent. The soul must learn to turn away from distraction and gather itself into peace. Yet the silence that receives God is not an achievement to possess. It is an openness in which your own striving grows quiet, and the divine Word may be spoken without obstruction.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Inhale:** *I release my striving*
>
> **Exhale:** *Let Your work unfold in me*
